# Community validation

Research status: desk research completed October 22, 2026; no student interviews, surveys, or live usability sessions have been conducted by this repository. The synthetic dataset is not evidence of a community problem. No improvement in real-world participation is claimed.

## What primary sources support

[Rutgers–New Brunswick Clubs and Organizations](https://newbrunswick.rutgers.edu/student-life/clubs-organizations) lists over 500 organizations and directs students to **The Arc**, powered by Suitable, for involvement and events. [Student Centers and Activities](https://sca.rutgers.edu/campus-involvement/student-organizations) also identifies The Arc as the current involvement portal. These sources support the scale and local relevance of organization discovery, but do not prove that an additional platform is needed.

The project PDF refers to getINVOLVED and scattered external channels. Treat fragmentation, difficulty finding beginner-friendly clubs, and willingness of officers to maintain another site as hypotheses. Do not assume The Arc lacks a feature merely because it is not visible publicly. Validate through a permitted student account and user interviews; no NetID integration or scraping is implemented here.

## Initial population and scope

Focus on New Brunswick freshmen, transfer students, and commuters exploring unfamiliar organizations, with 3–5 willing club officers who can verify their own content. Recruit volunteers with informed consent. Keep raw responses out of Git; collect no student ID, schedule detail, or sensitive identity information. Report anonymous, aggregated observations.

## Five-minute student interview

1. Tell us about the last time you tried to find a club or event. Which tools did you use?
2. What information did you need before deciding to attend? What was hard to find?
3. How do you keep track of meetings from multiple organizations?
4. Have you decided against attending? What influenced that choice?
5. Try a relevant discovery task in your current tool, then in the prototype. Where did you get stuck?

## Officer interview

1. Where do you publish event details today, and how long does an update take?
2. How do students ask about eligibility, fees, and beginner experience?
3. Who is authorized to edit your information, and who could verify this listing?
4. Would you maintain a separate profile? What would make that worthwhile?
5. Create an event in the prototype. What is missing or confusing?

## Task study and honest reporting

Counterbalance which interface each participant uses first to reduce learning effects. Use the same permitted real dataset and comparable task difficulty. Example tasks: find an open beginner event on a preferred campus, locate joining requirements, save two non-conflicting events, and publish a changed meeting location as an officer. Do not ask participants to enter private class timetables.

Record task success, elapsed seconds, number of corrections, and a 1–5 relevance/ease rating. Ask participants to explain their choices. Report participant count, recruitment, consent, task definitions, timing method, failures, medians and ranges, and quotations only with permission. Separate synthetic retrieval measurements from this user study. A bookmark or RSVP is not confirmed attendance.

## Data permission and freshness

Obtain written officer authorization for a profile, logo, description, FAQ, and events. Record source URL, permission scope, verifier, and verification date. Do not mark a fixture verified. Confirm details before publication and provide reporting/correction paths. Private Instagram, Discord, GroupMe, and university accounts must not be scraped. No external data integration is claimed.
